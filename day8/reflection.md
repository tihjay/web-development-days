The most difficult concept in this course was understanding how to design systems that continue working during traffic spikes. Early in the course, I focused mainly on functionality and whether a feature worked correctly. However, system design requires thinking about scale, performance, and reliability at the same time. Concepts such as caching, load balancing, and database transactions were initially challenging because they involved understanding how many users could access a system simultaneously and what could go wrong under heavy load. I overcame this by practicing capacity-estimation exercises and drawing architecture diagrams for different systems. Breaking large systems into smaller components made the concepts easier to understand.

Based on the feedback I received throughout the course, I would improve the scalability section of my capstone project. While the core functionality was well defined, I could provide more detail about handling traffic growth, reducing database load with caching, and protecting critical operations with queues and transactions. I would also improve my discussion of trade-offs by explaining why one design choice was selected over another.

Next, I plan to learn more about distributed systems and cloud architecture. Topics such as microservices, message queues, database replication, and monitoring will help me build systems that can support larger numbers of users while remaining reliable and efficient.
